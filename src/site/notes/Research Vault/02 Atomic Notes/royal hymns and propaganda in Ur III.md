“In this phase, royal hymns began to appear, modelled on earlier hymns of the gods. This was a clear outcome of the deification of the kings of Ur. Royal hymns were usually written in the first person (Text 9.5). Kings spoke in a self-laudatory and self-celebratory tone, indicating that they had not completely taken over the role of the gods. This new form of kingship propaganda complemented royal inscriptions. In the latter, the main focus was on the kings’ military victories and building activities. In the hymns, kings emphasised their talents, which were celebrated through anecdotes that had little to do with those accomplishments recorded in the inscriptions.” ([Liverani, 2014, p. 167](zotero://select/library/items/LX5B2JDD)) ([pdf](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/B4QW3W57?page=192&annotation=YW97HWGQ))
